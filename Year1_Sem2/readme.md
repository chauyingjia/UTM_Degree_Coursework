## 🖥 Computer Organization & Architecture

### Course Overview

This course is basically about what goes on inside a computer at the hardware level. We covered how the processor works, how memory is organised, how data moves around, and how instructions get executed. It answers the question most people never think to ask, what actually happens when you run a program?

### Reflection

Before this course, I treated computers like a black box. I knew how to write code but had no idea what the machine was doing with it. Learning about things like the CPU cycle, memory hierarchy, and instruction sets made me think differently about performance and efficiency. It was one of those subjects that feels very theoretical at first but starts making sense the more you code.

---

## 👤 Human Computer Interaction

### Course Overview

HCI is about designing technology that is actually easy and pleasant to use. We looked at how people interact with interfaces, what makes a design good or frustrating, and how to evaluate whether a system works well for its users. Less about coding, more about thinking from the user's perspective.

### Reflection

This was probably the most different course from what I expected in a CS programme. I thought it would feel out of place, but it actually gave me a new way to look at software. Good code that is hard to use is still bad software. Learning about usability and user needs made me more aware of design decisions in everything I build, even small things like naming buttons or organising a layout.

---

## 📊 Probability & Statistical Data Analysis

### Course Overview

This course introduces the mathematical side of working with data. We went through probability, distributions, hypothesis testing, regression, and how to interpret results properly. It is one of the more maths-heavy courses but very directly relevant to data engineering and analytics work.

### Reflection

Honestly, this course was tough at the start because the notation and concepts were quite abstract. But once I got used to thinking in terms of probability and distributions, it started to make a lot of sense. As someone studying data engineering, understanding statistics is not optional, it shows up everywhere from data analysis to machine learning. I am glad I went through this early rather than picking it up later on my own.

---

## 💻 Programming Technique II

### Course Overview

This is the continuation of Programming Technique I, but with a bigger shift in how we write code. The focus moved to object-oriented programming. I learn things like classes, objects, inheritance, and encapsulation. We also touched on data structures and more advanced problem-solving approaches.

### Reflection

The jump from procedural to object-oriented thinking took me a while to get used to. At first, writing classes felt like extra steps for no reason. But once I started working on bigger programs, I understood why structure matters. OOP makes code easier to manage and scale, and that lesson has stuck with me in every project since. This course probably changed how I code more than any other in Year 1.

---

## 📝 Overall Reflection

Semester 2 was where I started connecting the dots between different areas of computing. Hardware, statistics, programming, system design and user experience on the surface they seem unrelated, but they all feed into each other. I came out of this semester with a much clearer picture of what software development actually involves, and more importantly, what kind of engineer I want to become.
